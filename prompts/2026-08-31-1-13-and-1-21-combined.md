## 3. Order of work

**1.13 first, then 1.21.** Two reasons:

1. **1.13 carries the one genuinely risky refactor.** Task 2 asks you to extract
   `buildListWhere(query, user)` out of `TicketsService.list()` so the export and

   silently for every user. Do it while the suite is fresh in your mind, and if
   the extraction is anything more than a mechanical move, **stop and report**

2. **1.21 Task 1 is a pure extraction** (`runOnce()` on the SLA worker) whose
   proof is that existing SLA specs stay green. That is a cleaner thing to do

**Commit after each card, separately.** Two commits, not one — the planner
verifies each card on its own, and a problem in one must be revertable without
losing the other. Commit by explicit path, after reading `git status --short`.

## 6. Security — the non-negotiables from both cards

Restated because they are the two places where a mistake would matter:

**1.13 — the export must never widen access.** It reuses
`buildTicketAccessFilter` and `scopeReportQuery`. No bypass, no team override, no
`includeDeleted` flag. Integration cases 3 and 4 are the proof, and they are not
optional. **Spreadsheet injection** is a live risk because the file is opened in
Excel by definition: a value beginning `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` must be prefixed with
`'` (case 5).

**1.21 — `OwnerGuard` on both routes.** Running retention with dry-run off
deletes data; running the scheduler enqueues automation across every team.
TEAM_ADMIN and LEAD get 403, asserted by tests. The snapshot reports **states,
never values** — same rule as `/api/health/ready`: no connection strings, no
secrets, no setting values beyond on/off and intervals.
